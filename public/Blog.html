How much does it cost to build an ADU in Los Angeles?

July 23, 2024·8 min read·By the econstruct team

Accessory Dwelling Units are the fastest way to add livable square footage — and real value — to a Los Angeles property. But the first question every owner asks is the same: what will it actually cost? The honest answer is a range, and the range is driven by decisions you make early. Here's how we break it down.

The short answer: the typical range

For a detached, ground-up ADU in Los Angeles, most projects land between $250 and $400 per square foot, all-in. A garage conversion runs lower because the shell already exists; a custom detached unit on a hillside lot runs higher. The total budget is the sum of two buckets: soft costs and hard costs.

Soft costs: design, permits, and approvals

Soft costs are everything that happens before a shovel hits dirt. They're easy to underestimate and they protect the rest of the budget:

  • Architectural and structural design, plus Title 24 energy compliance
  • Survey, soils report, and civil work on sloped or constrained lots
  • City plan check and permit fees through LADBS
  • School fees and utility connection fees where applicable

Clear preconstruction planning here is what keeps the schedule honest later. We map the full scope before pricing so there are no surprises at permit.

Hard costs: the build itself

Hard costs are the physical construction — and where most of the budget lives:

  1. Site work & foundation — grading, concrete, and any retaining required by the lot.
  2. Framing, roofing & envelope — the weather-tight shell.
  3. MEP — mechanical, electrical and plumbing, including the utility runs to the main house.
  4. Finishes & fixtures — flooring, cabinetry, counters, appliances and the details that set the feel.
The single biggest cost variable isn't finishes — it's the utility tie-ins and the site. Know your lot before you set a budget.

Utilities are the wildcard

Running a new sewer lateral, upgrading the electrical panel, or extending water and gas across a property can move a budget by tens of thousands of dollars. On a flat lot with a panel near the build site, it's modest. On a hillside lot with a long run, it's a line item that deserves its own attention. We price this honestly up front rather than discovering it mid-build.

Where you can — and can't — save

You can save by converting an existing garage, choosing a simple rectangular footprint, and standardizing finishes. You can't save by skipping design coordination or cutting corners on the foundation — those decisions cost far more to fix later. The goal is a unit that's permitted cleanly, built to code, and ready to rent or live in on day one.
