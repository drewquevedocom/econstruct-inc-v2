## How to Vet Any Contractor Before You Hire

Before signing with any residential contractor in Los Angeles — regardless of how they appeared on a list or how impressive their website looks — there are several verification steps that protect your investment. The most important is confirming an active CSLB license through the California Contractors State License Board's online lookup tool. A valid license tells you the contractor has passed trade exams, carries required insurance, and is bonded — all of which are financial protections for you as a homeowner. California law also caps the legal down payment a contractor can request at 10% of the project cost or $1,000, whichever is less; any contractor demanding a larger deposit upfront is violating state law. Beyond the license check, ask for the contractor's general liability and workers' compensation insurance certificates — not just their word that they have it — and verify that the policies are current. Request references from at least three projects completed in the past two years at a similar scope and budget to yours, and actually call those references. The questions that matter most are: Did the project finish on schedule? Did the final cost match the original estimate? How did the contractor handle unexpected conditions or changes?
