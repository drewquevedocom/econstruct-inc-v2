A fully permitted commercial project can still sit idle for months waiting on a utility connection, and this happens more often than most developers expect. Utility providers run on their own scheduling logic, and an issued building permit does not move a project up in that queue.

## Utility Timelines Run Independent of the Permit Process

The city permit process and a utility provider's service upgrade process are two completely separate systems. A project can clear plan check and receive its permit while an electrical service upgrade request submitted late is still sitting unscheduled, because the utility was never asked early enough to move in parallel with permitting.

## Electrical Service Upgrades Are the Most Common Bottleneck

Increased electrical load from new equipment, HVAC systems, or a change in occupancy often requires a service upgrade from the local utility, and that process typically needs to start with a load calculation and application well before construction begins. Waiting until permits are issued to request this upgrade is one of the most common preventable delays on commercial projects.

## Water and Gas Coordination

Water service upgrades are often needed to meet fire suppression system demand, particularly for larger commercial or industrial buildings, and gas service increases are common for commercial kitchens and manufacturing equipment. Both processes involve their own review and scheduling with the respective utility, separate from the building permit.

## Building Utility Coordination Into Preconstruction

The fix is straightforward but requires discipline: identify every utility touchpoint during early preconstruction, submit service upgrade requests as soon as load calculations are available, and track utility timelines alongside — not after — the city permit timeline. Treating utility coordination as a parallel workstream rather than an afterthought is what prevents a permitted project from sitting idle.
