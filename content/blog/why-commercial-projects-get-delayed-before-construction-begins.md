Most commercial construction delays that owners and tenants experience did not happen on the job site. They were decided weeks or months earlier, during preconstruction, and simply became visible once framing started. Understanding where these delays actually originate is the first step to avoiding them.

## Incomplete Scope Definition

The single most common cause of a slipping schedule is a scope of work that was not fully defined before pricing and permitting began. When a contractor prices against a vague description, every unresolved decision becomes a potential change order once construction is underway, and each change order adds schedule risk on top of the last one. A complete scope, including finishes, equipment, and explicit exclusions, takes longer to prepare up front but eliminates the majority of mid-project disputes and delays.

## Utility Coordination Started Too Late

Utility service upgrades for expanded electrical, gas, or water service commonly take 8 to 14 weeks from application to completion, and the process needs to be initiated with the utility the day a lease is signed, not the day construction starts. Projects that wait until the permit is issued to begin utility coordination routinely find themselves finished with construction and unable to open because the utility upgrade has not caught up.

## Underestimated Plan Check Timelines

Plan check and permitting timelines in Los Angeles vary significantly by jurisdiction and scope, and a schedule built around an optimistic best-case timeline instead of a realistic one is a schedule that will slip. Correction cycles, especially for health department or fire and life safety review, commonly add weeks that a naive schedule does not account for.

## Long-Lead Items Not Identified Early

Custom millwork, specialty equipment, imported finishes, and certain mechanical or electrical components can carry lead times of 12 weeks or more. If these are not identified and ordered during preconstruction, they become the critical path item that delays the entire project regardless of how efficiently the rest of construction proceeds.

## How Disciplined Preconstruction Prevents This

A structured preconstruction process addresses each of these risks before a contract is signed: scope is documented in enough detail to price and permit accurately, utility coordination begins immediately, plan check timelines are built on realistic jurisdiction-specific data, and long-lead items are identified and released for procurement early. None of this is complicated. It requires discipline and a contractor who treats preconstruction as a distinct phase with its own deliverables, not a formality on the way to breaking ground.
