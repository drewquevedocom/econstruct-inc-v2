Developers bringing a commercial project into California from another state consistently underestimate the same few things, and each one is expensive to discover mid-project rather than during initial planning. None of these mistakes are complicated to avoid, but they require adjusting assumptions built in a different regulatory environment.

## Underestimating Entitlement and CEQA Timelines

Many states do not have an equivalent to California Environmental Quality Act review, and developers who have never budgeted schedule time for it are consistently surprised by how long discretionary entitlement can take in California, particularly in dense urban jurisdictions. This has to be researched and planned for before a project timeline is set, not discovered after acquisition.

## Misjudging Title 24 Compliance Costs

California's Title 24 accessibility and energy standards are often stricter than the codes developers are used to budgeting against, and construction estimates built on out-of-state cost assumptions frequently underestimate the true cost of California-specific compliance items.

## Treating California Permitting as One Statewide Process

California building code sets a baseline, but each city and county enforces its own amendments and plan check practices, and a process that worked in one jurisdiction does not automatically transfer to another, even within the same county. Developers who assume a single statewide process often build a schedule around the wrong jurisdiction's timeline.

## Underestimating Utility and Labor Market Differences

Utility service upgrade timelines and construction labor market conditions in California, particularly in Los Angeles, differ meaningfully from many other states, and both affect schedule and cost in ways that are easy to miss without local experience.
