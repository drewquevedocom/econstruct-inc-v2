Before a general contractor touches dock configuration, refrigeration engineering, or health department plan check, a food distribution or cold storage project starts with two decisions that shape everything downstream: which Southern California submarket, and what mix of ambient, refrigerated, and frozen space the operation actually needs. Get either wrong, and no amount of skilled construction fixes it after the lease is signed.

## Comparing Southern California Industrial Submarkets

Southern California food distribution corridor is not one market. It is several, each with real tradeoffs:

**Vernon, Commerce, and the LA industrial core.** Proximity to the urban customer base and an established food-industry labor pool, at a premium for available space and higher rent. Permitting runs through the City of Vernon or LA County depending on the parcel, both of which have direct experience with food facility plan check.

**Inland Empire - Fontana, Ontario, San Bernardino.** Larger available floorplates, newer building stock with higher clear heights and greater existing electrical capacity, at meaningfully lower rent. The tradeoff is longer last-mile drive times into the LA basin, which matters for operations on tight delivery windows.

**Long Beach and San Pedro.** Direct port access makes this the right choice for import-heavy operations, but space is limited and in high demand from broader logistics tenants beyond food distribution specifically.

**Santa Fe Springs and Southeast LA County.** A middle ground - reasonable proximity to LA, more available inventory than the urban core, established industrial infrastructure.

The right choice depends on actual freight pattern, customer base, and labor sourcing, not just headline rent per square foot.

## Ambient, Refrigerated, or Frozen: Deciding the Mix

The single most consequential space-planning decision is how much of the facility needs to be temperature-controlled, and at what temperature. This should be modeled against actual SKU mix and inventory turn before space planning locks in, not decided by a rule of thumb.

Ambient dry goods storage is the least expensive to build and operate. Refrigerated space, typically 32 to 41 degrees F, requires insulated envelope construction and continuous-duty refrigeration equipment. Frozen space, below 0 degrees F, requires heavier insulation, more powerful refrigeration, and in some cases subgrade heating to prevent frost heave beneath the slab. Retrofitting cold storage into a facility designed purely for ambient distribution is dramatically more expensive than designing the mix in from day one. Plan the ratio before touring buildings, not after signing a lease that cannot accommodate it.

## What Refrigeration Adds to the Construction Program

Once any part of the facility requires refrigeration, the construction program changes in ways that extend beyond the cold rooms themselves:

**Electrical service.** Commercial refrigeration is continuous-load equipment running around the clock. A facility with meaningful refrigerated or frozen space typically needs several hundred amps of dedicated three-phase service beyond what an ambient-only facility would require, often triggering a utility upgrade with SCE or LADWP on an 8 to 14 week timeline that should start the day the lease is signed.

**Structural loading.** Roof-mounted or yard-mounted refrigeration equipment adds point loads the structure must be designed or verified to carry, and frozen space may require subgrade insulation or heating to prevent frost heave.

**Title 24 energy compliance.** California Title 24 Part 6 energy code applies more stringent requirements to refrigerated warehouses than to standard industrial space, and non-compliance discovered during plan check is a common source of delay.

## Health Department and Multi-Jurisdiction Permitting

Any facility that receives, stores, or distributes food for commercial sale is subject to health department oversight from LA County Environmental Health, or the relevant county authority in the Inland Empire or Long Beach and San Pedro, in addition to standard building and mechanical permits. Because the submarkets above span multiple cities and counties, the permitting authority, and the plan check timeline, changes depending on where the facility is sited. Confirm the process required in that specific jurisdiction before finalizing a site, not after.
