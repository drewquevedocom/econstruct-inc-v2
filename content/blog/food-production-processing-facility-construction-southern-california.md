Food production and processing facilities carry a distinct set of construction requirements beyond a standard industrial building, driven by sanitation standards, health department approval, and tight coordination with process equipment. Understanding these requirements early avoids redesigning a shell that was built for general industrial use.

## Sanitary Construction Materials

Food production spaces typically require coved wall-to-floor transitions, non-porous and washable wall finishes, and ceiling systems that can withstand wash-down cleaning. These are not upgrades that can be easily added after a standard tilt-up or steel shell is built — they need to be specified in the original construction documents.

## Health Department Review Runs Alongside Building Plan Check

County or state health departments typically review facility layout, equipment clearances, handwashing station placement, and floor drainage independently of standard building plan check, and both approvals are generally required before a certificate of occupancy is issued. Submitting to both agencies with coordinated drawings avoids duplicate correction cycles.

## Coordinating Process Equipment Early

Process equipment vendors often drive structural loading, utility capacity, and drainage requirements that need to be locked in before the building shell design is finalized. Bringing the equipment vendor into preconstruction discussions early prevents a building that has to be retrofitted around equipment specifications discovered after permitting.

## When a Hood or Fire Suppression System Applies

Some food production processes involve cooking equipment that triggers a Type 1 hood and fire suppression requirement, which is its own distinct, heavily regulated scope; others involve only mixing, packaging, or cold processing and do not. Identifying which category a facility falls into early avoids either over-building an unnecessary system or missing a required one during plan check.
